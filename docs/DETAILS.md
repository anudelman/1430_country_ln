and a render always forgets.

Read your piece's section **and** the **[GLOBAL RULES](#global-rules)** at the bottom. Global rules
are defaults you apply without being told; the piece section only lists deviations and specifics.

Axes per CONVENTIONS: **+X = plan-right/east**, **+Z = toward the street/south**, backyard = **−Z**.
All heights are **to center, above finished floor (AFF)** unless stated.

## `exterior-front`
*Photos: `straight_on_view_of_house_from_street.png`, `front_leftside_of_house.png`, `front_rightside_of_house.png`*

### A. Wall & ceiling furniture
- **Round louvered gable vents** — the signature element. Dark charcoal, **~24" diameter**,
  louvered with a bug screen behind. One in the upper wall left of the entry projection, one in
  the recessed entry wall at ~7' AFG. Also a small **oval/round vent** high on the left wing.
- **House numbers** "1430" — dark metal, ~4" tall, modern sans-serif, mounted on the siding
  band to the right of the entry, roughly 6'6" AFG, slightly irregular letter spacing.
- **Gutters + downspouts** — dark charcoal-brown 5" K-style gutter with a rounded downspout at
  each outside corner; downspouts strapped at ~3 points, discharging to a black plastic elbow
  onto a splash block in the mulch.
- **Garage** — 16'0" sectional door, painted the same warm gray as the siding, flush/recessed
  long-panel style, **no windows**, black weatherstrip at the jambs, rubber bottom seal with a
  visible dirt line. **No exterior sconce** above the garage door in view.
- Electric meter / service head is on the far right (`front_rightside_of_house.png`) — small gray
  box, conduit up to the eave.

### B. Trim & construction
- **Siding:** horizontal lap, **7–8" exposure**, painted warm gray-greige with a lightly
  rough-sawn/embossed texture. Butt joints staggered; a couple of joints show a slightly darker
  caulk line.
- **Trim, fascia, soffit, window frames, corner boards, porch beam:** all a **darker
  charcoal-brown** than the siding. Two-tone, high contrast. Corner boards ~4" wide.
- **Roof:** low-slope hip with wide overhangs (~24"), charcoal-gray **architectural asphalt
  shingle** with visible shadow-line variation between courses.
- **Second-floor entry projection** oversails the porch; its underside is the dark soffit.
- **Windows:** dark-framed, no grilles/muntins in the front picture units; one tall narrow
  3-lite vertical unit beside the entry.
- **Porthole window** — round/oval fixed window with a white-ish interior frame is visible
  through the second-floor wall on some elevations (see `upper-hall`).

### C. Finish details
- Walkway/porch: **irregular-rectangular bluestone flagstone**, ~1.5" thick, gray-blue with
  rust/tan iron staining, laid ashlar with **tight sand/dirt joints (~3/8")**, not mortared.
- Stone step riser is a stacked stone course, ~6" rise, edges chipped and slightly out of level.
- Driveway: broom-finish concrete, control joints on a ~10' grid, faint tire staining.

### D. Objects present
Mature multi-trunk deciduous tree in a **black mulch ring** in the front lawn; rounded
boxwood/euonymus balls (3–4) in beds along the facade; a dense privet/euonymus hedge mass right
of the entry; black mulch beds edged with a few fieldstone boulders. **Nothing else** — no cars,
no bikes, no hose reel, no potted plants at the street.

### E. Wear & imperfection
Lawn is mown with faint mower stripes but has **thin/dry patches near the driveway edge** and a
slightly ragged edge where turf meets mulch. A few darker weathered siding boards. Sidewalk slabs
are subtly mismatched in tone and one is tilted.

### F. Windows look out on / backdrop
Clear blue sky with thin cirrus. Neighbor left: beige siding + brick, gable roof. Neighbor right:
cream/tan stucco with a chimney. Street with a concrete curb and public sidewalk, canopy trees
overhanging from both sides, dappled shade on the lawn. Late-morning to midday sun, warm.

strong contrast between deep porch shadow and blown-out sunlit stone at the walkway.

# GLOBAL RULES

Apply these everywhere unless your piece section says otherwise.

### G1. Electrical devices
- **Every device is white "decora" (rocker/rectangular) in a white plate**, screwless, throughout
  the house — **except `primary-bath`, where the plates on the wood slat wall are BLACK.**
- **Outlets: 15" AFF to center**, vertical orientation, one per wall minimum on field walls.
- **Backsplash/counter outlets: ~44" AFF, HORIZONTAL orientation** (kitchen and baths).
- **Switches: 46–48" AFF to center**, beside the latch side of each door opening. Gang counts
  seen: 1-gang most bedrooms/baths; **2-gang** at kitchen openings, gym, and living/nook walls.
- At least one plate per room should be **~0.5–1° off plumb**. Never make them all perfect.

### G2. HVAC — three distinct register families, use the right one
| location | type |
|---|---|
| **First floor** | **Floor** registers set in the oak, **bronze/oak-finish metal, 4"×10" or 4"×12"**, ~4" out from the baseboard, usually under a window |
| **Second floor** | **Floor** registers set in the carpet, **white metal, 4"×10"**; plus **white wall supply grilles ~12"×6" mounted HIGH near the ceiling** in `primary-bed` and `family` |
| **Returns** | **White metal, ~14"×8" to ~20"×12", horizontal blades, mounted HIGH on the wall** (top within ~10" of ceiling) in `upper-hall` and `breakfast-nook`; **small white ~10"×6"** low on walls in bedrooms and basement |
| **Basement** | White rectangular diffusers in the drywall bulkheads; small white wall grilles both high and low |
- **One ornamental white filigree/scroll cast grille** exists — on the `upper-hall` balcony
  fascia. Unique; do not repeat it elsewhere.
- **Bath exhaust:** white square ~10" grille with a concentric diamond louver pattern
  (`basement-bath` shows it clearly).

### G3. Doors and hardware
- **Interior doors: white painted 6-panel (colonial raised panel), 6'8" × 1.75".**
- **All interior door hardware is MATTE BLACK / oil-rubbed bronze levers** (a few closet/utility
  doors get **black round knobs**). No brushed nickel on doors anywhere.
- **3 hinges per door, matte black**, at ~7", 40", 66".
- **Closets: white 6-panel BYPASS sliding doors** on a top track with a white head jamb and
  **round recessed black finger pulls (~2" dia.)**. Closet rods/shelves are not visible in any
  photo — the closets are all closed. Do not model interiors unless a door is open.
- **Front door:** near-black, round rain-glass light ~24" dia., reeded center panel, black
  handleset at 36" + round deadbolt at 48".
- **No door stops are visible in any photo.** Add small black baseboard-mounted stops sparingly,
  or omit.

### G4. Trim
- **Baseboard: white satin, 5.25" tall** on floors 1 and 2 (5.5" in `dining`), flat field with a
  **small eased/ogee cap**, ~5/8" thick. **Basement: ~4.5".** **No quarter round / shoe mold
  anywhere** — the base sits directly on oak, tile and carpet with a caulk line.
- **Casing: white flat, ~3.5" wide** with a slight backband edge. **Window heads get a mitered
  cap with a small drip crown** projecting ~1" (clearest in `dining` and `breakfast-nook`).
- **Window stool projects ~1.25" past the casing** with a bullnose/eased edge; **apron ~2.5"
  tall** below it, dying into the casing legs. Exception: the tall `foyer` windows have
  essentially no stool.
- **Crown molding: white, 3-piece built-up, ~4.5–5" drop** (cove/ogee + flat frieze + small cap).
  **PRESENT in:** `dining`, `family`, `breakfast-nook`, `living`, and on the hall/foyer side of
  the kitchen opening.
  **ABSENT in:** the kitchen ceiling itself, `foyer`, `mudroom`, `bed-first`, all baths, **the
  entire second floor**, and the entire basement. Getting this map wrong is an instant tell.
- **Boxed white square columns (~10")** occur in the kitchen/nook/living open plan, with the
  **baseboard mitered around the base**.
- **`family` is SUNKEN one step (~7")** from the kitchen/hall level — white painted riser, oak
  bullnose tread.

### G5. Flooring and its direction
| level / room | material | direction |
|---|---|---|
| Entire first floor (`foyer`, `kitchen`, `breakfast-nook`, `dining`, `living`, `family`, `bed-first`) | **Red oak strip, ~3.25", natural/amber, satin poly** | **NORTH–SOUTH (front-to-back, parallel to Z)** |
| `mudroom` | **Light blonde LVP/engineered plank ~7"** | **EAST–WEST** (perpendicular to the oak) — with a **transition strip at the hall doorway** |
| `upper-hall` and second-floor hall | Red oak, same product | **EAST–WEST** |
| All second-floor bedrooms + `primary-bed` | **Beige/greige loop carpet, fine linear texture** | **nap/vacuum banding parallel to the window wall** |
| `basement-rec` | **Beige/tan berber loop, pin-dot texture** | banding across the long dimension |
| `basement-gym` | **Dark charcoal speckled loop** | transition strip at the doorway |
| `bath-first`, `basement-bath` | Beige 12×12 porcelain, straight-set | parallel to the walls |
| `bath-second` | Marble-look ~12×24, white grout ~1/16" | parallel to the walls |

  dark mineral streaking. A uniformly-toned oak floor is the single most common render tell here.
- Every flooring change happens **at the door opening**, under the closed door position, with a
  visible metal or wood transition strip.

### G6. Lighting hardware
- **Recessed cans: 6" aperture, white trim, white baffle**, spaced **4'–6' on center** in a loose
  grid that follows the room shape, and set **~2'–3' off the walls**. They are **NOT** on a
  perfect symmetrical grid — offset a few.
- **Every fixture in the house is ON in every photo, in full daylight.** Render them lit.
- Fixture inventory: kitchen island = 2 black cone pendants with brass chain; `dining` = black +
  brass sputnik globe chandelier; `upper-hall` = brass dandelion starburst sphere; all three
  second-floor bedrooms = **identical black drum "fandelier" with an LED ring**; `mudroom`,
  `primary-bed` vestibule, `basement-rec` = white flush LED discs; `basement-gym` = one 2×4
  troffer in a suspended grid; baths = linear LED bars (**black** upstairs, **chrome** in
  `bath-first`).

### G7. Staging — the most important rule
**This house is almost entirely EMPTY.** Across all 47 photos the only movable objects are:
- the **coir "welcome home" doormat** at the front door,
- the **LG washer and dryer** in the mudroom,
- the **kitchen appliances** (range, dishwasher, refrigerator) and the **wet-bar wine fridge**,
- the **toilets**,
- the **magnifying makeup mirror** in `primary-bath`.

There is **no furniture, no rug, no artwork, no plant, no towel, no soap, no curtain, no bedding,
no gym equipment, and nothing on any countertop anywhere in the house.** An over-furnished render
fails the blind test exactly as badly as an under-detailed one. Build the trim, build the
devices, build the registers — and leave the rooms bare.

### G8. Windows and glazing
- Interior sash and casings are **white** — except `bedroom_second_floor_kid-2`, which has
  **honey-oak-stained interior sash**, and the `sunroom` + rear glass box, which are **dark
  charcoal-brown timber/frames**.
- **Plantation shutters (white, 3.5" louvers, divider rail)** occur in `foyer`, `bed-first`, and
  behind the entry sidelights. Nowhere else. **No curtains, blinds or drapes anywhere else**
  except the mudroom door's mini-blind.
- Casement hardware is **black**: fold-down crank operators and lock levers. **No screens** on
  any operable window in these photos.
- Window views must be built: every one looks onto **mature green canopy, mown lawn, weathered
  wood picket fence, black mulch beds with river-cobble edging, and a neighbor's roofline** —
  brick red, cream stucco, blue-gray siding, or gray shingle depending on the elevation.
  Bright and near-blown but **never clipped to pure white**, always with a green/blue colour cast.

### G9. Wear and imperfection budget (per room, minimum)
1. One device plate ~1° off plumb.
2. Visible caulk line where trim meets wall, tile meets counter, and base meets floor — slightly
   uneven, slightly glossy.
3. Board or tile colour variation, and at least one visibly odd-width cut tile at a wall return.
4. Grout width variation (target ±25%) and tone variation between wall and floor.
5. A faint drywall taping/seam shadow on at least one long wall in raking light.
6. A soft light gradient across every large wall — never a flat wash.
7. Carpet vacuum/nap banding; oak satin sheen that smears anisotropically toward the light.
8. Dust and smudges on glass, fingerprints on stainless.
